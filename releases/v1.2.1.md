## What's new

**Red that came in from last month looks different from red you made this month.** Until now a fund that was over in December stayed "over" in January, February and March, even if you spent exactly what you planned — and the Review list, the month recap and the garden all counted it the same way. Now a fund that's only short because of an earlier month gets a quiet grey "carrying" chip instead of a red "over" one, Review lists it under "Carried in from earlier months" rather than "Needs attention", a plant that stayed inside this month's plan blooms at month end, and one line under your month's Income / Spent / Net always tells you the total still on the books. Nothing is hidden and no number changed — "over" just means over *this month* now. Watching the deficit go down month over month is the point.

**"Free to move" waits until the month is over.** A Basic fund used to be called free the moment its first charge posted, which made money look spare that a second charge still needed. Now Basic and Pacing funds are free to move only once the month is done; a savings goal only once its target month is over. A Fixed fund (one bill on a schedule) is still free as soon as its bill posts.

**Pause a fund this month.** Open any spending fund and click Pause: its planned amount becomes $0 for this month only and comes back when you start the next one. Good for a one-paycheck month. Typing a planned amount unpauses it.

**Chips say why.** A fund can now show both its status ("over $57") and what explains its planned amount ("overridden", "paused", or "full" for a build-up pot that's reached its cap) — the second chip isn't hidden behind the first any more.

**Goals in plain words.** A savings goal's tooltip explains its monthly ask ("$58.33 = $50.00 toward the goal + $8.33 a month to make back $100.00 from before"), and if you plan less than it asks, the chip and the fund panel tell you where you'll land ("At $150.00 a month you'll have $1,200.00 of $3,000.00 by March — $1,800.00 short").

**Three small warnings.** Moving money out of a fund that would go below zero now asks first ("Transfer anyway"). Changing an income amount tells you how much the tithe moved, with one Ctrl+Z to undo both. And a fixed fund whose bill came in higher than it was set up for offers to update the total and top up the difference, instead of calling it overspending.

No changes to how your data is stored. Mac: Settings → Check for updates still opens the downloads page; in-place updating on Mac is waiting on a Mac tester.
